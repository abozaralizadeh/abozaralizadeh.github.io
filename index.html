01 / Now

By night, it's one human and a crew of AI agents.

After work I build products on my own. My only team is a group of AI agents that I direct from a single control chat.

They research, build, review each other's work and write the docs. I decide what to build, test everything on my own phone and own every commit. It turns out one person can ship a lot this way.

◀◀ This story runs backwards: the newest work first, then how I got here.

Put the AI only where the uncertainty is. Everything else should be boring code you can test.

How I build

  1. 01

    Build, review, ship.

    One agent builds a feature, another reviews it like a tough critic, a third polishes it. All before I install anything.

  2. 02

    Measure everything.

    Every feature ships with automated tests, and every claim is checked against real data before it goes out.

  3. 03

    One session per project.

    Every product keeps its own long-lived agent session with its whole history. One Mission Control chat steers them all.

  4. 04

    Frugal by design.

    No team to pay, no ad budget. Free cloud tiers, a Claude subscription and a lot of tests.

  5. 05

    Build in public.

    Public repos, a live trading dashboard and write-ups on Medium. Anyone can watch the work as it happens.

  6. 06

    Ship the story too.

    Every product ships with a build diary, demo films and social posts, made mostly by my own agents and skills.

03 / October 2026 · CronAI

Say it, don't cron it.

CronAI · open source

A neural network inside a TypeScript file.

A plain-English schedule picker for any website. A tiny 75k-parameter model lives in the source code and only fixes typos. A strict compiler writes the cron line, so it can't make anything up. On a typo benchmark, exact matches go from 16.9% to 93.8%. It also runs the schedules in AISM².

04 / September 2026 · Two products, one night

One night in September, I started two products a minute apart.

Two agent sessions, opened sixty seconds apart: a booking platform for clinicians and a marketplace for group games. Three weeks later both were running, each with its own tests, legal research, films and build diary.

Praxamo · live at praxamo.it

Praxamo: your practice, online.

Their own site, their own name.

Every Italian clinician gets a professional website and online booking at their-name.praxamo.it. No marketplace in between: the patients you bring stay yours.

A dentist, a GP, a cardiologist.

Eight templates and eleven colour schemes, all contrast tested, so each practice gets a site that actually looks like theirs.

Regulation is the architecture.

Every page is checked against Italian health advertising law before it can go live. Write "the best practice in Milan, guaranteed results!" and it says no.

Book without an account.

Patients book as guests on their phone. Confirmations and reminders arrive in the practice's own name and colour.

A human looks first.

Practices run their agenda, patients and availability from one console. And nobody goes live until a human (me) has checked them against the official registers.

Tested like it matters.

1,504 automated tests and 77 tenant isolation checks. Even the marketing film is a test: if the booking doesn't confirm while it records, the recording stops.

Visit praxamo.it

The film uses an AI voice and sample data.

Partyta · in demo, payments simulated

scroll to play

Partyta: any group game, one seat at a time.

Paintball, laser tag, softair and VR need 10 to 24 players. Nobody sells those games seat by seat, so you end up chasing friends for days.

On Partyta, venues publish open games and you join alone or with friends. Your card is only held, and it's charged only when the game reaches its minimum. If it never fills, nobody pays.

The logo is the product: a ring with one free place, and a coral dot filling the gap.

// what's inside

A player app and website from one codebase, a console for venues, a group chat for every game, an iPhone build and 231 automated tests, in Italian and English.

05 / July 2026 · AISM²

AISM² · open source

An agent that runs social media.

Write a brief and a schedule. When it's time, an agent reads the brief and its own memory, researches the topic, makes an image or a short Sora 2 video, writes the caption and posts to Instagram, X, YouTube or TikTok. Or it waits for approval.

It runs in production every day and manages my own X, Instagram and YouTube. One example is @genaicomicbook, the Instagram page of the ComicBook: AISM² posts every new episode and reel there by itself.

Schedules are written in plain English with my own CronAI widget, and for every instruction I choose how much freedom the agent gets: post on its own, wait for my approval, or just rehearse in dry-run mode.

06 / December 2025 · trAIde

trAIde · open source · built in public

AI trading agents, with real money on the line.

A research agent scans about 700 futures contracts and the news. A trading agent decides. A supervisor talks to me on Telegram. And plain code, not the model, owns the stops.

Since 30 September I've been running an experiment: Jev vs Luna. Jev, a second decision model, trades next to Luna, the original agent, on the same account and behind the same safety gates. Each is scored only on its own calls, and the public dashboard compares their decisions side by side.

Code enforces survival. The model owns opportunity.
